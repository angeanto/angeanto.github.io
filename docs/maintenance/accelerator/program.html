The 8 Live Sessions

¶

The program includes 8 live sessions.

Each session is 1 hour and includes teaching, discussion, implementation guidance, and office-hour style support.

Program Structure


  1. Program orientation and platform context: Understand the product, access, learning path, and expectations.

  2. Databases, warehousing, SQL, and data modeling foundations: Understand data structures, joins, windows, modeling concepts, and warehouse thinking.

  3. Pipelines, dbt, Git, and analytics engineering foundations: Learn how repeatable analytics workflows are versioned, tested, and documented.

  4. BI communication, storytelling, and executive insight delivery: Move from dashboards to decision-ready communication.

  5. Data governance, data quality, documentation, and scaling analytics: Learn why definitions, ownership, lineage, and trust matter.

  6. Analytics and BI in the AI era: Use AI as a productivity layer while keeping human judgment, validation, and accountability.

  7. Business thinking, domains, KPIs, stakeholders, and strategy: Learn how senior analysts connect analytics work to business decisions.

  8. Data analyst careers in 2026, CVs, interviews, AI, and standing out: Learn how to explain this experience credibly in your CV, interviews, and career story.

    AI Use Cases

    ¶

    AI is part of the modern analytics ecosystem, but it is not presented as magic.

    Participants will explore AI as a serious productivity layer:

    • conversational BI exposure with Wren AI,
    • AI-assisted thinking for dbt model development,
    • prompt design for analytics workflows,
    • reviewing and validating AI-generated analysis,
    • understanding where AI helps and where human judgment is still essential.

    AI can help produce faster. Senior analysts must still decide what matters, validate what is true, communicate clearly, and own the recommendation.

    Governance And Metadata

    ¶

    Senior analysts need to understand trust.

    That means more than writing SQL.

    They need to understand:

    • metric definitions,
    • ownership,
    • lineage,
    • documentation,
    • data quality expectations,
    • governance workflows,
    • how metadata helps teams find and trust data.

    The platform includes OpenMetadata so participants can see how governance and metadata fit into the analytics ecosystem.

    Why The Cohort Is Limited To 10 People

    ¶

    The first cohort has only 10 seats. This is not artificial scarcity. It is intentional design.

    There are four reasons:

    1. Infrastructure stability The course uses a real data platform. A smaller cohort protects reliability and makes the experience smoother.

    2. Small cohort learning This is not a mass video course. The goal is a focused group of serious learners.

    3. Better discussion and feedback Fewer participants means deeper questions, better office hours, and more useful support.

    4. Premium experience The product is designed to feel careful, human, and practical.
